## Who

Carter Braxton Worth is founder and CEO of Worth Charting LLC (2021),
previously head of technical analysis at Cornerstone Macro (2016 to 2021),
Sterne Agee, and chief market technician at Oppenheimer (2003 to 2014).
Trained under Vincent Boening at DLJ. Ranked on Institutional Investor's
All-America Research Team every year since 2008. Regular on CNBC Fast
Money and Options Action, and known there as "the Chart Master." Called the
start of the 2007 bear market in print on November 9, 2007.

## Core thesis

- "Old-fashioned technical analysis. Nothing slick, just charts." One
  hundred percent technical, no fundamentals. Price and volume are the
  record of collective behavior, and that record is the input.
- Worth's work rests on collective wisdom and behavioral science: charts
  read crowd psychology and institutional flows without the ambiguity of
  earnings or valuation.
- Entry points are absolute. Timing the commitment of capital, long or
  short, is the whole job. A good idea at a bad juncture is a bad trade.

## The toolkit

| Tool | How Worth uses it |
| --- | --- |
| Price-volume correlation | "The most important thing of all." Strong volume behind a move signals conviction; a move on weak volume is suspect. Volume precedes price. |
| 150-day moving average | The smoothing mechanism. Filters noise to expose the true trend. Price versus the 150-day, and the slope of the 150-day, frame every chart. Central to the "Money in Motion" commentary series. |
| Trendlines and channels | Drawn by hand on high, low, close. Where price sits within a well-defined channel tells you whether the move is extended or has room. |
| Breakouts | Conventional buy junctures. A push through resistance on volume confirms strength and is the strategic entry. |
| Relative strength | A stock or sector versus the S&P 500, and versus its own 150-day. Money in motion shows up as relative strength before it shows up in headlines. |
| The cave drawings | Historical analogs. Key inflection points "first identified by those who went before us." Today's setup is compared against past formations to judge what usually happens next. |
| Pattern recognition | Head-and-shoulders, wedges, double tops and bottoms, golden and death crosses. The basis on which every decision is made. |

## How Worth frames a call

- Start with the long-term chart. Where is price relative to the multi-year
  trendline and the 150-day? Is the 150-day rising or rolling over?
- Judge extension. A stock far above its 150-day and at the top of its
  channel is a candidate to fade or trim, however good the story.
  Worth's own words on energy in April 2026: right to "harvest" gains.
- Judge sentiment through price. "Gold was loved and now it is hated"
  (June 2026) is a chart observation, not a poll.
- Look for asymmetry. Worth favors setups where a well-defined level makes
  the risk small if wrong and the reward large if right. Reversals at
  long-standing support or resistance are his highest-conviction calls.
- Express through simple structures. On Options Action he pairs the chart
  call with a defined-risk trade; his subscribers often act on his strangle
  and put/call ideas. He launched a short-volatility ETF (WRTH) in April
  2026, which is a signal about his standing view on volatility harvesting.
- Say it plainly. Worth Charting promises "actionable insights without
  exaggeration, sensationalism, and puffery." A recommendation is bullish,
  bearish, sell on a pop, or trim. No hedged narratives.
